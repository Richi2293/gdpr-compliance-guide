## 1. Legal Framework

### 1.1 GDPR — Regulation (EU) 2016/679

General regulation on the protection of personal data, directly applicable in all Member States.
Relevant articles: 4(11), 5, 6, 7, 12-14, 15-22, 26, 28, 30, 37, 77.

- Official text: [eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)

### 1.2 ePrivacy Directive — 2002/58/EC (amended by 2009/136/EC)

Specific rules for cookies and tracking technologies (lex specialis with respect to the GDPR).
Key article: 5(3).

- Official text: [eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/oj)

**Important note:** The proposed ePrivacy Regulation, which was intended to replace this Directive, was formally withdrawn by the European Commission in February 2025. The ePrivacy Directive therefore remains in force and continues to constitute the legal basis for the regulation of cookies and tracking technologies, despite its framework dating back to 2002 (last updated 2009). See Section 6 for ongoing developments (Digital Omnibus Package).

### 1.3 EDPB Guidelines (European Data Protection Board)

- **Guidelines 05/2020 on consent** (version 1.1, 4 May 2020): clarifications on consent, cookie walls, and scrolling.
  [edpb.europa.eu](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-052020-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en)
- **WP260 rev.01 — Guidelines on Transparency** (11 April 2018): requirements for privacy notices and privacy policies.
  [ec.europa.eu](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/622227)
- **Guidelines 03/2022 on Deceptive Design Patterns** (14 February 2023): principles against dark patterns in consent collection interfaces. Formally addressed to social media platforms, but the principles (Stirring, Overloading, Skipping, Hindering, Fickle, Left in the Dark) are applicable by analogy to consent banners.
  [edpb.europa.eu](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-032022-deceptive-design-patterns-social-media_en)
- **Guidelines 2/2023 on Technical Scope of Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive** (adopted 16 October 2024): clarify the technical scope of Art. 5(3) ePrivacy, confirming that it applies to tracking pixels, tracking URLs, local processing, Web Storage API (localStorage, sessionStorage), and any technology that accesses or stores information on the user's device.
  [edpb.europa.eu](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-22023-technical-scope-art-53-eprivacy-directive_en)
- **Guidelines 03/2025 on the interplay between the DSA and the GDPR** (12 September 2025): clarifications on how providers of intermediary services must apply the GDPR in the context of the Digital Services Act. Relevant for sites that integrate third-party services (ad targeting, content moderation). Under public consultation until 31 October 2025.
  [edpb.europa.eu](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/documents/public-consultations/2025/guidelines-32025-interplay-between-dsa-and-gdpr_en)

### 1.4 CJEU Judgment C-673/17 (Planet49)

Judgment of the Court of Justice of the EU of 1 October 2019: pre-ticked checkboxes do not constitute valid consent. Key case law reference.

### 1.5 European Accessibility Act — Directive (EU) 2019/882

Directive on the accessibility of products and services, applicable from 28 June 2025. It extends and supersedes the scope of national accessibility laws, imposing accessibility requirements for digital services offered to consumers, including websites and mobile applications of private economic operators.

- Reference technical standard: EN 301 549 v3.2.1 (harmonised with WCAG 2.1 level AA)
- Official text: [eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)

**Important note:** The EAA applies to all economic operators providing digital services to consumers in the EU. Consent banners, privacy notices, and preference management panels fall within the scope of the EAA's accessibility requirements.

### 1.6 Munich Regional Court Judgment (20 January 2022, Az. 3 O 17493/20)

Judgment of the Landgericht München establishing the unlawfulness of loading Google Fonts from external servers (fonts.googleapis.com), as it transmits the user's IP address to Google without a valid legal basis. Damages of €100 awarded for violation of the right to informational self-determination. Key reference for the obligation to self-host third-party static assets.
