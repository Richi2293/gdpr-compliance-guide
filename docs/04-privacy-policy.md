## 4. Privacy Policy (Information Notice under Articles 13–14 GDPR)

### 4.1 Obligation to provide an information notice

**Requirement:** The data controller must provide the data subject with a privacy notice at the time personal data are collected. The information notice is mandatory and must be provided free of charge.

**Source:** Art. 13 GDPR (data collected from the data subject); Art. 14 GDPR (data not collected from the data subject); Art. 12(5) GDPR (free of charge).

**Notes:** For a website, Art. 13 is the primary reference (data are collected directly from the user). Art. 14 applies if the controller receives data from third-party sources. The obligation does not apply if the data subject already has the information (Art. 13(4) GDPR). For manifestly unfounded or excessive requests, the controller may charge a reasonable fee or refuse to act, providing reasons for the refusal (Art. 12(5) GDPR).

---

### 4.2 Presentation of the information notice

**Requirement:** The information notice must be provided in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language. It may be provided in writing or by other means, including electronic means.

**Source:** Art. 12(1) GDPR; EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01.

**Notes:** The EDPB recommends:
- **Layered notice approach**: first layer with essential information, subsequent layers for details. Recommended for digital contexts.
- **Push notices (just-in-time)**: information provided proactively at the exact moment of collection (e.g., tooltip next to a form field).
- **Pull notices**: information accessible on demand by the user (e.g., dashboard, "learn more" links, tutorials).
- **Dashboard**: control panel for managing preferences and consents.
- **Icons**: may be used as a complement (not a substitute) to text (Art. 12(7) GDPR).

The EDPB also recommends an evidence-based approach, testing the comprehensibility of the notice with real users.

---

### 4.3 Facilitating the exercise of rights

**Requirement:** The controller must facilitate the exercise of data subject rights (Articles 15–22 GDPR). The information notice must clearly indicate the practical means for exercising each right.

**Source:** Art. 12(2) GDPR; EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01.

**Notes:** Provide concrete details: a dedicated email address, an online form if available, postal address. The process must not be unduly burdensome. A single point of contact for all rights is acceptable, provided it is clearly indicated.

---

### 4.4 Response timeframes for requests

**Requirement:** The controller must respond to data subject rights requests within 1 month of receipt. This period may be extended by a further 2 months for complex or numerous requests (informing the data subject within 1 month). If the request is made electronically, the response must be provided electronically.

**Source:** Art. 12(3) GDPR.

**Notes:** It is good practice to state these timeframes in the information notice. If the controller does not act on the request, they must inform the data subject within 1 month, providing the reasons and the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority or seeking a judicial remedy (Art. 12(4) GDPR).

---

### 4.5 Verifying the identity of the requester

**Requirement:** The controller may request additional information to confirm the identity of the data subject exercising their rights, where the controller has reasonable doubts.

**Source:** Art. 12(6) GDPR.

**Notes:** Identity verification must not be used as a barrier to the exercise of rights. The controller may refuse to act only if it can demonstrate that it is unable to identify the data subject.

---

### 4.6 Identity and contact details of the controller

**Requirement:** The information notice must state the identity and contact details of the data controller and, where applicable, of its representative.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(a) GDPR.

**Notes:** Company name, registered office, tax identification number, email, and any telephone numbers. If the controller is established outside the EU, the designated representative under Art. 27 GDPR must be indicated.

---

### 4.7 DPO contact details

**Requirement:** If the controller has appointed a Data Protection Officer (DPO), the information notice must include their contact details.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(b) GDPR; Art. 37 GDPR (mandatory appointment cases).

**Notes:** It is not necessary to name the DPO, but at least one contact channel must be provided (e.g., a dedicated email address such as dpo@domain.com). Appointment is mandatory for: public authorities, large-scale processing of special categories of data, regular and systematic monitoring on a large scale.

---

### 4.8 Purposes and legal basis for processing

**Requirement:** For each processing purpose, the information notice must state: the specific purpose and the corresponding legal basis under Art. 6(1).

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(c) GDPR; Art. 6(1) GDPR.

**Notes:** Applicable legal bases:
- **(a) Consent**: for marketing, profiling, non-essential cookies
- **(b) Performance of a contract**: for service delivery, account/order management
- **(c) Legal obligation**: for tax and accounting compliance
- **(d) Vital interests**: rarely applicable to websites
- **(e) Public interest**: for public bodies/exercise of official authority
- **(f) Legitimate interest**: for website security, fraud prevention (the specific interest must be stated — Art. 13(1)(d))

---

### 4.9 Legitimate interests pursued

**Requirement:** If the legal basis is the legitimate interest of the controller or a third party (Art. 6(1)(f)), the information notice must specify those legitimate interests.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(d) GDPR.

**Notes:** Legitimate interest requires a balancing test against the rights and freedoms of the data subject (Legitimate Interest Assessment — LIA). It is good practice to document the balancing test and make it available upon request, as part of accountability (Art. 5(2) GDPR).

---

### 4.10 Data recipients

**Requirement:** The information notice must indicate the recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, distinguishing between data processors and independent controllers.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(e) GDPR; Art. 28 GDPR (data processors).

**Notes:** Distinguish between:
- **Data processors** under Art. 28 GDPR (e.g., hosting provider, email services, CRM): process data on behalf of the controller, governed by a contract
- **Independent controllers** (e.g., Google for Analytics, advertising platforms): process data for their own purposes
- **General categories** (e.g., "judicial authorities, if required by law")

It is not necessary to name every individual recipient if categories are indicated, but greater transparency is preferable.

---

### 4.11 Joint controllership

**Requirement:** If two or more controllers jointly determine the purposes and means of processing (joint controllers), the essential content of their arrangement must be made available to the data subject. The information notice must indicate the respective roles and points of contact.

**Source:** Art. 26 GDPR.

**Notes:** This applies when the controller operates with joint controllers (e.g., shared platforms, integrated services). The data subject may exercise their rights against each of the joint controllers, regardless of the internal arrangement. If not applicable to the specific project, this section may be omitted from the information notice.

---

### 4.12 International data transfers

**Requirement:** If data are transferred to a third country or an international organisation, the information notice must indicate: (a) the country or organisation, (b) the existence or absence of an adequacy decision, (c) the appropriate safeguards under Articles 46–47, or derogations under Art. 49.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(f) GDPR; Articles 45–49 GDPR.

**Notes:** Common safeguards: Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs), EU-US Data Privacy Framework (adequacy decision of 10 July 2023). Always verify the current status of adequacy decisions and providers' adherence to applicable frameworks. For the risk of invalidation of the EU-US DPF ("Schrems III") and the recommendation to have SCCs as a supplementary safeguard, see Section 3.13.

---

### 4.13 Data retention period

**Requirement:** The information notice must state the data retention period or, if it is not possible to determine it in advance, the criteria used to establish it.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(a) GDPR.

**Notes:** Specify for each purpose/data category. Examples: "browsing data: session duration"; "contact form data: 12 months from last communication"; "tax data: 10 years under legal obligation"; "marketing data: until consent is withdrawn".

---

### 4.14 Right of access

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the user of the right to obtain from the controller: confirmation of whether data are being processed, access to the data, and information about purposes, categories, recipients, retention period, rights, source, and automated decision-making.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR; Art. 15 GDPR.

**Notes:** The controller must provide a copy of the data being processed. The first copy is free of charge; for further copies, a reasonable fee may be charged (Art. 15(3)).

---

### 4.15 Right to rectification

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the right to obtain rectification of inaccurate data and completion of incomplete data without undue delay.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR; Art. 16 GDPR.

---

### 4.16 Right to erasure (right to be forgotten)

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the right to obtain erasure of data when: (a) they are no longer necessary, (b) consent is withdrawn, (c) there is an objection with no overriding legitimate grounds, (d) unlawful processing, (e) legal obligation, (f) data collected from a child in relation to information society services.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR; Art. 17 GDPR.

**Notes:** Exceptions to erasure: freedom of expression and information, legal obligation, public health interest, archiving/statistical research, establishment or defence of legal claims.

---

### 4.17 Right to restriction of processing

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the right to obtain restriction of processing when: (a) accuracy is contested, (b) processing is unlawful but the data subject opposes erasure, (c) the controller no longer needs the data but the data subject requires them for legal claims, (d) an objection has been lodged pending verification.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR; Art. 18 GDPR.

**Notes:** During restriction, data may only be stored (not actively processed), except with the data subject's consent or for the establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims.

---

### 4.18 Right to data portability

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the right to receive their data in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, and to transmit those data to another controller.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR; Art. 20 GDPR.

**Notes:** This applies only when: (a) the legal basis is consent or performance of a contract, AND (b) the processing is carried out by automated means. For a website with only a contact form, applicability is limited; for SaaS applications and apps with user accounts, it is generally relevant.

---

### 4.19 Right to object

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the right to object at any time to processing on grounds relating to their particular situation (where the basis is legitimate interest or public interest). For direct marketing, the right to object is unconditional.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR; Art. 21 GDPR.

**Notes:** Art. 21(4) requires that the right to object be presented **clearly and separately** from any other information, "at the latest at the time of the first communication with the data subject". For direct marketing (Art. 21(2)–(3)): the data subject may object at any time without providing reasons, and processing must cease immediately. Art. 21(5) provides that in the context of information society services, the right to object may be exercised by automated means using technical specifications.

---

### 4.20 Right relating to automated decision-making and profiling

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling under Art. 22(1) and (4), providing meaningful information about the logic involved, the significance, and the envisaged consequences.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(f) GDPR; Art. 22 GDPR.

**Notes:** This applies only if there are decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal effects or similarly significantly affect the data subject. Exceptions: necessity for a contract, authorisation by law, explicit consent. In all cases, the data subject has the right to: human intervention, to express their point of view, and to contest the decision. If no such processes exist, it is sufficient to state this explicitly.

---

### 4.21 Right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority

**Requirement:** The information notice must inform the data subject of the right to lodge a complaint with a competent supervisory authority.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(d) GDPR; Art. 77 GDPR.

**Notes:** The data subject may lodge a complaint in the Member State of their habitual residence, place of work, or place of the alleged infringement.

---

### 4.22 Right to withdraw consent

**Requirement:** If processing is based on consent, the information notice must inform the data subject of the right to withdraw consent at any time, specifying that withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent given prior to withdrawal.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(c) GDPR; Art. 7(3) GDPR.

**Notes:** It must be clearly indicated how to exercise withdrawal (e.g., link to cookie preference panel, email to the controller, account dashboard).

---

### 4.23 Mandatory or optional nature of data provision

**Requirement:** The information notice must indicate whether the provision of data is a statutory or contractual requirement, or a requirement necessary to enter into a contract, and the consequences of failing to provide the data.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(e) GDPR.

**Notes:** Example: "Providing data in the contact form is optional. Failure to provide data will prevent us from responding to your request." For data necessary for service delivery: "Provision is necessary for the performance of the contract. Failure to provide data will prevent activation of the service."

---

### 4.24 Further processing for a different purpose

**Requirement:** If the controller intends to process data for a purpose other than the one for which the data were collected, the controller must inform the data subject before the further processing, providing all relevant information.

**Source:** Art. 13(3) GDPR.

**Notes:** The information notice may anticipate further purposes from the outset, or the obligation arises at a later stage through a supplementary notice.

---

### 4.25 Notification to recipients

**Requirement:** The controller must communicate any rectification, erasure, or restriction of processing to each recipient to whom the data have been disclosed, unless this proves impossible or involves disproportionate effort. The data subject has the right to be informed about those recipients, upon request.

**Source:** Art. 19 GDPR.

**Notes:** Art. 19 is not formally listed among the mandatory contents of Art. 13. However, it confers an independent right on the data subject (to be informed about recipients of rectifications/erasures) that is good practice to mention in the information notice for completeness and transparency.

---

### 4.26 Direct marketing and soft spam

**Requirement:** If data are used for direct marketing via email, SMS, or similar electronic communications, prior consent is required. Exception — "soft spam": the controller may send commercial communications for products or services similar to those already purchased by the customer, without consent, provided the data subject is informed of the right to object.

**Source:** Art. 21(2)–(3) GDPR (unconditional right to object to direct marketing); Art. 13 ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (soft spam exemption for existing customers).

**Notes:** The information notice must specify: (a) whether data will be used for marketing, (b) the legal basis (consent or soft spam exemption), (c) the unconditional right to object to direct marketing. The right to object to marketing must be presented clearly and separately (Art. 21(4) GDPR).

---

### 4.27 Double opt-in for newsletters and mailing lists

**Requirement:** For subscription to newsletters and commercial mailing lists, the double opt-in procedure is strongly recommended (and in practice required): (1) the user fills in the form, (2) receives a confirmation email with a link, (3) must click the link to complete the subscription.

**Source:** Art. 7(1) GDPR (burden of proof for consent); EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent.

**Notes:** Double opt-in is not formally mandatory by law, but it is the most reliable method to demonstrate that consent was actually given by the data subject (and not by a third party who entered their email address). Without double opt-in, the controller may be unable to satisfy the burden of proof under Art. 7(1) GDPR. The subscription form must contain: (a) a summary notice or link to the full information notice, (b) an unticked consent checkbox, (c) indication of the specific purposes (informational newsletter, commercial offers, etc.).

---

### 4.28 Date of last update

**Requirement:** The privacy policy must clearly indicate the date of the last update, visible to the user.

**Source:** Art. 5(2) GDPR (accountability); EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01.

**Notes:** Maintain historical versions of the notice with publication and modification dates. Document the reasoning behind choices of legal bases and balancing of interests. Version control of the information notice is a key accountability tool.

---

### 4.29 Accountability principle

**Requirement:** The controller must be able to demonstrate compliance with all processing principles, including transparency. The information notice itself is one of the tools for demonstrating compliance.

**Source:** Art. 5(2) GDPR.

**Notes:** Accountability elements relevant to the information notice: (a) retention of historical versions with dates, (b) documentation of the Legitimate Interest Assessment for each processing activity based on Art. 6(1)(f), (c) records of processing activities under Art. 30 GDPR, (d) Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) under Art. 35 GDPR where applicable for high-risk processing (e.g., large-scale profiling). See Section 5 for details on organisational obligations.

---

### 4.30 Specific requirements for data collection forms

**Requirement:** Every form that collects personal data (contact, registration, newsletter, quote request, job application) must: (a) link to or display the privacy notice before data collection, (b) include an unticked consent checkbox for each purpose requiring consent, (c) clearly distinguish mandatory from optional fields, (d) indicate the consequences of failing to complete mandatory fields.

**Source:** Art. 13 GDPR (information notice at the time of collection); Art. 7(2) GDPR (distinguishable consent); Art. 13(2)(e) GDPR (mandatory/optional nature); Recital 32 GDPR (unticked checkboxes).

**Notes:** Technical requirements for form verification: (a) a link to the privacy notice must be visible before the submit button, (b) consent checkboxes must have `checked="false"` or no `checked` attribute (never pre-ticked), (c) checkbox text must indicate the specific purpose (e.g., "I consent to the processing of my data to receive commercial communications" — not a generic "I accept the privacy policy"), (d) asterisk or indication for mandatory fields, (e) `required` attribute only on fields genuinely necessary for the purpose (data minimisation principle), (f) separate consents for different purposes (e.g., one checkbox for "responding to the request" and a separate one for "newsletter"). Consent for the performance of the requested service (e.g., "responding to your contact request") does not require a separate checkbox if the legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) — the information notice is sufficient.

---

### 4.31 Social login and OAuth authentication

**Requirement:** If the website offers authentication through third-party providers (Google Sign-In, Facebook Login, Apple Sign-In, GitHub OAuth, etc.), the information notice must specify: (a) which data are received from the provider (name, email, profile photo, etc.), (b) the purpose of processing those data, (c) the legal basis, (d) whether data are shared with the provider or with third parties.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(c)(e)(f) GDPR; Art. 14 GDPR (if data are received not directly from the data subject); Art. 26 GDPR (possible joint controllership).

**Notes:** Social login creates a bidirectional data flow: the provider sends data to the website (name, email) and the website potentially sends data to the provider (login event, activity). If the provider uses the data for its own purposes (e.g., Facebook for advertising profiling), this constitutes joint controllership under Art. 26 GDPR (cf. CJEU C-40/17, Fashion ID). The information notice must: (a) list the data received from each provider, (b) specify whether the login creates a local account or is entirely delegated to the provider, (c) indicate how to revoke access (both on the website side and on the provider side), (d) specify the safeguards for extra-EU transfers if the provider is established outside the EEA.

---

### 4.32 E-commerce and transactional data

**Requirement:** For e-commerce websites, the information notice must specifically cover: (a) billing and shipping data, (b) payment data, (c) order history, (d) data necessary for tax and accounting compliance.

**Source:** Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of contract); Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR (legal obligation for tax compliance); Art. 13(2)(a) GDPR (retention period).

**Notes:** Typical legal bases for e-commerce: (a) performance of the contract (Art. 6(1)(b)) for order processing, shipping, returns/refunds management, (b) legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)) for invoicing and retention of tax documents, (c) consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) for marketing, shared wishlists, reviews, (d) legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) for fraud prevention. Payment data (card number, CVV) are generally not processed by the controller if a payment gateway is used (e.g., Stripe, PayPal) — specify in the information notice that payment data are processed directly by the gateway as a processor or independent controller, with a link to its privacy notice.

---

### 4.33 Geolocation and location data

**Requirement:** The collection of geolocation data (GPS, IP-based geolocation, Wi-Fi positioning, Bluetooth beacons) requires a specific information notice and, in most cases, the user's consent.

**Source:** Art. 4(1) GDPR (personal data); Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent); Art. 9(1) GDPR (if combined with data revealing sensitive information); Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (access to information on the device).

**Notes:** Precise geolocation (GPS) is a particularly intrusive form of personal data that may reveal sensitive information (visits to places of worship, healthcare facilities, trade union premises). Requirements: (a) clear notice about the precision of the location data collected, (b) a specific and proportionate purpose (e.g., "show the nearest restaurant" — do not collect location for generic analytics), (c) explicit consent before activating geolocation, (d) ability to withdraw consent and disable location tracking, (e) indication of the retention period for location data. IP-based geolocation (city-level) is less intrusive but must still be disclosed in the information notice.

---

### 4.34 Language of the privacy notice

**Requirement:** The privacy notice must be drafted in the language or languages of the users to whom the website or service is directed.

**Source:** Art. 12(1) GDPR; EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01 (paras. 11–12).

**Notes:** For multilingual websites: the information notice must be available in each language offered by the website. The translation must be professional and accurate — translation errors may render the notice non-compliant with the "clear and plain language" requirement. If the website is directed at children, the language must be further simplified (Art. 12(1) GDPR).

---

### 4.35 Practical facilitation of the exercise of rights

**Requirement:** In addition to listing data subject rights, the information notice must provide practical and concrete means for their exercise, minimising barriers.

**Source:** Art. 12(2) GDPR; EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01.

**Notes:** Recommended best practices: (a) a dedicated email address (e.g., privacy@domain.com) clearly indicated, (b) an online form for data subject rights requests, with structured fields (type of right, description of request, identifying information), (c) a user dashboard with self-service options for the most common rights (data download, account deletion, preference management). The process must not require special tools or technical knowledge. Using identity verification as a barrier to the exercise of rights is prohibited (Art. 12(6) GDPR).

---

### 4.36 Web server logs

**Requirement:** Web server access logs (access log, error log) contain personal data — in particular the user's IP address, browser user agent, visited URLs, timestamps, and HTTP status codes. The processing of these data must be covered by the privacy notice.

**Source:** Art. 4(1) GDPR (IP address is personal data — cf. CJEU C-582/14, Breyer); Art. 13(1)(c) GDPR (purpose and legal basis); Art. 13(2)(a) GDPR (retention period).

**Notes:** The information notice must specify: (a) that the website automatically collects browsing data (IP, user agent, URL, timestamp), (b) the purpose (system security, diagnostics, abuse prevention), (c) the legal basis (typically legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — with a documented balancing test), (d) the retention period (recommendation: max 90 days for security purposes; longer periods require specific justification), (e) whether the IP is anonymised and after what interval. Server logs are often managed by the hosting provider (e.g., AWS Amplify, CloudWatch) — verify that the provider is covered by an adequate DPA (see Section 5) and that logs are not retained for excessive periods. Logs must not contain data entered by the user in forms (e.g., passwords, personal data in POST request bodies) — configure the server to exclude logging of request bodies.

---

### 4.37 Territorial scope (Art. 3 GDPR)

**Requirement:** The GDPR applies to the processing of personal data: (a) in the context of the activities of an establishment of the controller in the EU, regardless of where the processing takes place; (b) of data subjects who are in the EU, by a controller not established in the EU, if the activities relate to the offering of goods or services or the monitoring of behaviour within the EU.

**Source:** Art. 3 GDPR; EDPB Guidelines 3/2018 on territorial scope.

**Notes:** For a website directed at the EU market, the information notice and consent banner must be presented to all visitors, regardless of their geographic location, because: (a) it is not possible to determine the user's location with certainty before data collection, (b) geo-blocking the notice or banner is risky and inadvisable, (c) a non-EU visitor may still be physically located in the EU. If the website also targets non-EU markets with their own privacy regulations (e.g., California — CCPA/CPRA, Brazil — LGPD, UK — UK GDPR), the information notice may need to cover those regulations as well. For most standard EU-focused websites, it is sufficient to apply the GDPR to all visitors without geographic differentiation.
