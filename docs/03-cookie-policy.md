## 3. Cookie Policy

### 3.1 Extended information notice (second layer) requirement

**Requirement:** The website must provide an extended cookie policy (second-layer information notice) containing all the information required by Articles 12 and 13 GDPR, specifically relating to the use of cookies and tracking technologies.

**Source:** Art. 12 GDPR; Art. 13 GDPR; EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01 (layered approach to transparency).

**Notes:** The cookie policy may be a dedicated section of the privacy policy or a separate document. In both cases, it must be reachable with a single click from the banner and via a permanent link in the footer.

---

### 3.2 Accessibility of the cookie policy

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must be accessible: (a) with a single click from the consent banner, (b) via a permanent link in the footer of every page of the website.

**Source:** EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent (layered approach and accessibility of information); Art. 12(1) GDPR (easily accessible).

**Notes:** The footer link must always be visible, regardless of whether the banner has been closed or not.

---

### 3.3 Identity and contact details of the controller

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must state the identity and contact details of the data controller and, where applicable, of its representative.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(a) GDPR.

**Notes:** For a company: company name, registered office, tax identification number, contact email. If the controller is established outside the EU, the designated representative under Art. 27 GDPR must be indicated.

---

### 3.4 DPO contact details

**Requirement:** If the controller has appointed a Data Protection Officer (DPO), their contact details must be included in the cookie policy.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(b) GDPR.

**Notes:** Appointing a DPO is mandatory for public authorities, large-scale processing of special categories of data, and regular and systematic monitoring on a large scale (Art. 37 GDPR). For SMEs with standard web activities, appointing a DPO is optional but recommended.

---

### 3.5 Purposes and legal basis for each cookie

**Requirement:** For each cookie or tracking technology, the cookie policy must state: (a) the purpose of the processing, (b) the applicable legal basis.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(c) GDPR; Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC.

**Notes:** For technical cookies, the legal basis is the exemption under Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive (strictly necessary). For analytics cookies treated as technical: the same exemption applies, subject to specific conditions (see Section 2). For profiling/marketing cookies: consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR. Legitimate interest is NOT a valid basis for profiling cookies (EDPB Guidelines 05/2020).

---

### 3.6 Cookie categories

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must classify the cookies used into distinct categories, making clear the coding criteria adopted for each one.

**Source:** Art. 12(1) GDPR (transparency); Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (distinction between exempt and non-exempt cookies); EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent (granularity).

**Notes:** The minimum categories are: (1) technical/necessary cookies, (2) analytics cookies (specifying whether treated as technical or subject to consent), (3) profiling/marketing cookies. The controller must enable the user to distinguish between the categories. The coding criteria for cookie identifiers must be made transparent in the information notice.

---

### 3.7 Detailed cookie table

**Requirement:** For each cookie, the cookie policy must indicate: name, provider/domain, purpose, retention period, type/category.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(c) GDPR (purposes); Art. 13(2)(a) GDPR (retention period).

**Notes:** Although no specific tabular format is prescribed, a table is the most practical solution to simultaneously satisfy the obligations to indicate purposes, recipients and retention periods. The table must be kept up to date.

---

### 3.8 List of third parties

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must identify all third-party data recipients, with links to their respective privacy policies. The list must be kept constantly up to date.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(e) GDPR.

**Notes:** Third parties may be reached via direct links to their policies or via a link to an intermediary site representing them. It is important to distinguish between third parties acting as data processors under Art. 28 GDPR (e.g. hosting, email services) and third parties that are independent controllers (e.g. Google for Analytics).

---

### 3.9 Data retention period

**Requirement:** For each processing operation (and therefore for each cookie), the data retention period must be indicated or, where not possible, the criteria used to determine it.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(a) GDPR.

**Notes:** Indicate the actual cookie duration (e.g. "24 hours", "2 years", "session") and, if data is stored server-side for a different period, specify that as well.

---

### 3.10 Data subject rights

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must inform the user of the existence of the following rights: access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), portability (Art. 20), objection (Art. 21).

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(b) GDPR.

**Notes:** A reference to the corresponding section of the privacy policy may be sufficient, provided it is easily accessible. See Section 4 of this document for details on individual rights.

---

### 3.11 Right to withdraw consent

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must inform the user of the right to withdraw consent at any time and specify how to exercise it (e.g. link to the preference management panel, link in the footer).

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(c) GDPR; Art. 7(3) GDPR; EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent.

**Notes:** It must be clearly stated that withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent given before the withdrawal.

---

### 3.12 Right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must inform the user of the right to lodge a complaint with the competent supervisory authority.

**Source:** Art. 13(2)(d) GDPR; Art. 77 GDPR.

**Notes:** The data subject may lodge a complaint in the Member State of their habitual residence, place of work, or place of the alleged infringement. National implementations may specify local supervisory authority details.

---

### 3.13 International data transfers

**Requirement:** If data collected through cookies is transferred outside the European Economic Area (EEA), the cookie policy must indicate: the destination country, the existence or absence of an adequacy decision by the Commission, and the appropriate safeguards adopted.

**Source:** Art. 13(1)(f) GDPR; Arts. 45-49 GDPR.

**Notes:** Relevant for services such as Google Analytics (data transferred to the USA). Verify whether the provider adheres to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (adequacy decision of 10 July 2023) or whether standard contractual clauses (SCCs) are used. Always verify the current status of adequacy decisions.

**Risk note on the EU-US DPF:** The Data Privacy Framework is formally valid, but the risk of invalidation ("Schrems III") is concrete: (a) the Latombe appeal (dismissed by the EU General Court on 3 September 2025) was appealed to the CJEU on 31 October 2025; (b) noyb/Max Schrems has announced a broader challenge; (c) the Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board (PCLOB), a key body in the DPF redress mechanism, has been effectively dismantled; (d) the DPF is based on Executive Order 14086 (Biden), which is unilaterally revocable. The CJEU has already invalidated two prior frameworks (Safe Harbor in 2015 and Privacy Shield in 2020). It is recommended to have standard contractual clauses (SCCs) in place as a supplementary safeguard, so as not to rely exclusively on the adequacy decision.

---

### 3.14 Browser-level cookie management instructions

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must provide instructions on how the user can manage cookies at the browser level, with links to the guides of the major browsers.

**Source:** Art. 12(1) GDPR (clear and accessible information); Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (user control over terminal equipment).

**Notes:** Include links to the guides for: Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, Opera. Specify that disabling cookies via the browser may impair the functionality of some website services.

---

### 3.15 Clear and accessible language

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must be drafted in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language.

**Source:** Art. 12(1) GDPR; EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01.

**Notes:** Avoid legalistic or incomprehensible technical language. Use short sentences, active voice, and avoid double negatives. If the website is also aimed at minors, the language must be particularly simple (Art. 12(1) GDPR).

---

### 3.16 Accessibility via assistive technologies

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must be usable via assistive technologies (screen readers, keyboard navigation) without discrimination.

**Source:** Directive (EU) 2019/882 (European Accessibility Act), applicable from 28 June 2025.

**Notes:** Structure the page with semantic headings, accessible tables (with `<th>`, `scope`, `caption`), adequate colour contrast, and resizable text. From 28 June 2025, the European Accessibility Act extends accessibility requirements to all consumer-facing digital services, including privacy notices and cookie policies. The reference standard is EN 301 549 v3.2.1, harmonised with WCAG 2.1 level AA.

---

### 3.17 Date of last update

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must clearly show the date of the last update, visible to the user.

**Source:** Art. 5(2) GDPR (accountability — demonstrating compliance over time); best practice.

**Notes:** Retain historical versions of the cookie policy with publication and modification dates, to demonstrate the evolution of compliance over time.

---

### 3.18 Classification of session and authentication cookies

**Requirement:** Session, authentication and security cookies must be correctly classified in the cookie policy. The following types fall under technical cookies (exempt from consent): session ID, CSRF token, authentication cookies (JWT in cookie, session token), load balancer cookies, security cookies (e.g. `__Host-` or `__Secure-` flag).

**Source:** Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (exemption for strictly necessary cookies).

**Notes:** Classification as "technical" requires the cookie to be strictly necessary to provide a service explicitly requested by the user. A session cookie that persists beyond the browser session or is used for additional purposes (e.g. analytics, unsolicited personalisation) loses its "technical" classification. In the cookie table, specify for each one: exact name, duration (`Session` or specific duration), security flags (`Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite`), and justification for the technical classification.

---

### 3.19 Cookie policy language

**Requirement:** The cookie policy must be drafted in the language or languages of the website's target users. For multilingual websites, each language version of the site must have a cookie policy in the corresponding language.

**Source:** Art. 12(1) GDPR ("clear and plain language"); EDPB Guidelines WP260 rev.01 (paras. 11-12).

**Notes:** The EDPB specifies that the information notice must be in the language of the target user, not only in the language of the country where the controller is established. A website in one language with a version in another language must have cookie policies in both languages. The translation must be accurate and not merely automated. For websites targeting multiple EU countries, ensure that country-specific information (e.g. competent supervisory authority, national provisions) is adapted.

---

### 3.20 Recommended cookie duration by category

**Requirement:** The duration of each cookie must be proportionate to its purpose and limited to the time strictly necessary. There is no universal maximum legal limit, but supervisory authorities assess the proportionality of duration relative to the declared purpose.

**Source:** Art. 5(1)(e) GDPR (storage limitation); Art. 13(2)(a) GDPR (obligation to state the retention period).

**Notes:** Recommendations by category, based on European supervisory authority practices:

| Category | Recommended duration | Notes |
|-----------|-------------------|------|
| Technical session cookies | Session duration (`Session`) | Must expire when the browser is closed |
| Authentication cookies | Session duration or max 30 days for "remember me" | Longer durations require justification |
| Consent storage cookies | 6 months | Consistent with the re-prompt period (Section 2.14) |
| Analytics cookies (treated as technical) | Max 13 months (CNIL recommendation) | The French CNIL indicates 13 months as maximum duration |
| Profiling/marketing cookies | Max 12-13 months (CNIL/AEPD recommendation) | Durations exceeding 13 months are considered disproportionate |
| Preference cookies (language, layout) | Reasonable duration (e.g. 12 months) | Proportionate to the benefit for the user |

The cookie table in the cookie policy (Section 3.7) must indicate the actual duration of each cookie. If the configured duration exceeds the recommendations, the controller must be able to justify the choice on proportionality grounds.
